01 · The upgrades

P2MR, and Dilithium 2.

These are the changes that matter. The public key stays off the chain while a coin rests. The spend uses a NIST lattice signature, Dilithium 2, the parameter set also called ML-DSA-44.

CRYSTALS-Dilithium, NIST FIPS 204, parameter set ML-DSA-44. A signature is 2,420 bytes. A public key is 1,312. This is the signature that replaces ECDSA on a mainnet spend.

02 · Why this level

Enough security for the chain that already exists.

Dilithium 2 is NIST security category 2, the class of a SHA-256 collision. Heavier parameters buy a margin this chain’s own proof of work does not have, and they spend the bytes the block was opened up to carry.

SHA-256
Proof of work is still SHA-256. A signature above that category does not make the ledger harder to rewrite.
The spend
A quantum computer needs the public key. P2MR shows that key at the moment of spend, so the signature covers the spend, not years of an exposed address.
The bytes
Dilithium 2 is already about 2,420 bytes, against about 71 for ECDSA. Dilithium 5, ML-DSA-87, is a larger signature for category 5. The 8 MB block and the one-minute target exist so Dilithium 2 fits.

03 · Critical mass

Our Satoshi is alive.

A new signature only helps coins someone can still move. Bitcoin already carries a lost supply, widely put near 10 percent, and Satoshi’s coins are in it. No one can sign for that stash, so it cannot follow a newer scheme. The migration leaves the mass behind.

Bitcoin

A silent stash

The founder coins have no living signer. When the network has to move coins onto the latest security, that supply stays where it is.

Bitcoin Quantum

A company that can sign

The founder is a company. It is alive, it carries a fiduciary duty, and the shareholder base is on the public record. When the signature standard changes, those coins can move. The chain keeps a critical mass on the latest security.

04 · What stayed Bitcoin

Same coins. Same machines.

Miners keep SHA-256. Holders keep a 21 million cap and a four-year halving. The chain is separate: bitcoin does not move onto it, and there is nothing to buy ahead of launch.

The argument, in five pieces.

The signature, the block, and the address. Each one is a short note you can read on its own.

  1. 01 From ECDSA to Dilithium What the signature change requires: opcodes, size, wallets, and running both schemes.
